# How it works today: mom with high blood pressure, from hospital to home

Researched 2026-11-08. Sources at the bottom. Where hospitals differ, it says "varies".

## The big picture

Most of the danger happens AFTER she goes home, and that is exactly when nobody is watching.

- Blood pressure after birth usually gets WORSE between day 3 and day 6. That is after most moms leave the hospital (usually day 2 to 4).
- In one study, the first very high reading came at a median of day 6.
- In Mississippi, 32% of pregnancy deaths happen in the first 6 weeks after birth, and 38% between 6 weeks and 1 year.
- 82% of Mississippi pregnancy deaths were judged preventable. High blood pressure and heart problems are top causes.

## Step by step, today

### 1. Pregnancy (before the hospital)
- Prenatal visits check her blood pressure.
- If it is high, she gets a label: chronic high blood pressure, gestational high blood pressure, or preeclampsia.
- In Mississippi, almost half the counties have no birth doctor or birthing hospital. Many moms drive far for these visits.

### 2. In the hospital (labor, birth, 2 to 4 days of recovery)
- Nurses check her blood pressure every few hours.
- If it hits 160/110 or higher, hospitals follow a standard safety plan: treat it with medicine within 30 to 60 minutes.
- Doctor may start a blood pressure medicine (common ones: labetalol, nifedipine).
- Everything is written in the hospital's record system (Epic, Cerner, etc.).

### 3. Discharge (the handoff, the weakest moment)
What the nurse does, usually in one rushed session:
- Goes over warning signs out loud. The standard sheet is called POST-BIRTH: Pain in chest, Obstructed breathing, Seizures, Thoughts of hurting self or baby, Bleeding, Incision not healing, Red or swollen leg, Temperature high, Headache that won't go away or vision changes.
- Hands her paper instructions and prescriptions.
- Tells her to make an appointment. For high blood pressure moms, guidelines say a blood pressure check within 72 hours (if severe) or 7 to 10 days.
- Some hospitals give her a home blood pressure cuff. Many do not. Varies.
- Mississippi's quality group (MSPQC) runs projects to improve this exact handoff, so the state knows it is a problem.

What goes wrong:
- She is exhausted, has a newborn, and gets a pile of paper. Much of it is forgotten.
- The appointment is often not booked before she leaves.
- 50% to 70% of high blood pressure moms do NOT get their 7 to 10 day blood pressure check.

### 4. Home, days 1 to 10 (the danger zone)
- Today, the system depends on HER to notice danger and call.
- Some hospitals make ONE nurse phone call 1 to 3 days after discharge. There is a standard script for it that asks about her pain, bleeding, blood pressure, headache, swelling, vision, mood, AND the baby: feeding, wet diapers, yellow skin, safe sleep. It is one call, done by hand, and only if a nurse has time. Varies.
- Signs she might miss or ignore: headache, blurry vision, swelling, shortness of breath. Many moms think "it's normal after birth."
- If it gets bad: she goes to the emergency room (often far away in rural Mississippi), or she gets readmitted, or worse.
- Big hospitals (Penn, UF, UMMC) run remote programs: daily readings by text, app, or Bluetooth cuff, watched by nurses. These need a smartphone, internet, or special equipment, and they reach a small share of moms.

### 5. Follow-up visit
- Guidelines (ACOG): every mom should have contact with her doctor within 3 weeks, and a full visit by 12 weeks.
- In reality, many moms miss it: no ride, no childcare, work, distance.
- The doctor often sees her with no idea what happened at home since discharge. There are no readings, just what she remembers.

### 6. Up to 1 year
- Since 2023, Mississippi Medicaid covers moms for 12 months after birth (it used to be 60 days).
- Women with pregnancy high blood pressure have higher heart disease risk for life, but long-term follow-up is weak.

## The gaps (where we fit)

1. Nobody checks her during days 3 to 10, which is the peak danger window.
2. The one phone call, when it happens, is manual, one-time, and depends on nurse time.
3. Remote programs that exist need smartphones, internet, or equipment, which rural moms often lack.
4. The doctor at the follow-up visit has no data from home.
5. Discharge instructions are paper that gets lost.

## What our product changes

- Discharge notes go in, and the AI builds her personal call plan (her medicines, her danger numbers, her schedule).
- A voice call every day on ANY phone. It asks the same questions the nurse script asks, about her and the baby.
- Danger rules follow the standard numbers (see below). The nurse is alerted right away, and the mom is told what to do.
- Every answer builds a timeline the doctor sees before the follow-up visit.

## Standard danger numbers (from published programs and guidelines)

- 140/90 or higher: elevated, nurse should review.
- 160/110 or higher: severe, needs treatment fast.
- 180/120 or higher, or chest pain, bad headache, vision changes, shortness of breath: go to the emergency room now.

Our build should treat these as settings the hospital can change, and the nurse makes the medical decision.
